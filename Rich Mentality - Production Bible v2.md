# Rich Mentality - Production Bible v2

## 2. Premise and message

The video asks what happens when everyone starts thinking like an owner and a builder. Its answer: ambition builds a society only while it stays tied to what other people need.

**Premise Lock (one sentence):** overnight, every adult adopts the seven habits below as their goals and behaviour; nobody receives money, land or qualifications, and laws, institutions, existing wealth, prices and technology start unchanged.

**What a rich mentality means here**

- Financial literacy and careful money management
- Long-term thinking and delayed gratification
- Investing and building assets
- Spotting opportunities and starting things
- Developing valuable skills
- Creating products and services other people want
- Seeking financial independence and lasting wealth

**What it is not:** greed, status obsession, materialism, or wanting money without creating value.

**Time span shown:** the first months, then roughly ten years later. Nothing in the video claims poverty ends or inequality disappears.

**The dividing line (a story device, not a lecture):** ambition is productive when it passes three tests.

1. Does it create something of value for another person?
2. Can the person stop, rest and be satisfied?
3. Does it leave room for the health and relationships the person would choose to keep?

The primary character mostly passes these tests and is pushed toward failing them. A supporting character fails the second and third tests early. Neither is a villain.

**Central message (two candidates, you choose):**

- A: Ambition is a tool. It builds when it serves people and hollows out when it replaces them.
- B: Wanting more only becomes wealth for a society when it becomes something other people need.

Both avoid "money is bad" and "ambition is noble". Final wording is written with the script, not now.

## 3. Cause and effect

The story follows one chain: people save and build, spending dips, businesses flood in, competition and labour shortages follow, and the human cost shows up last. Each link below is rated, and the video only states as fact what the sources support.

**Ratings**

- **Plausible:** a well-understood mechanism or a real precedent supports it.
- **Uncertain:** possible, but it depends on conditions the story cannot settle.
- **Speculative:** a storytelling choice, not researched as a prediction.

| Link | Rating | Why it follows | Evidence checked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Everyone saves and budgets more | Premise | This is the stipulated change, not a forecast. | Studies disagree on whether education alone changes behaviour, so the story never claims it does. |
| 2. Spending dips at first, shops slow down | Plausible | If many households save more at once, sales fall before investment catches up (the paradox of thrift). | A textbook short-run idea. It assumes weak demand, and saving can fund investment, so the size of the dip is uncertain. |
| 3. Many people try to start businesses | Plausible | Ambition plus a goal of ownership raises start-up attempts. | US business applications hit about 5.5 million in 2023, a record at that point. An application is not a business, and only about a third were flagged as likely to hire. |
| 4. Many new businesses fail or stay tiny | Plausible | More entrants chase the same customers. | In US data, about 80 percent of new establishments survive year one and about half survive five years. These count locations, not firms. |
| 5. Competition squeezes prices and profit | Plausible | Copycat stalls split the same demand. | Reasoned from the mechanism, no study needed. |
| 6. Employee jobs become hard to fill and low pay rises | Plausible | When many people leave jobs to build their own, employers compete for the rest. | After 2020 in the US, pay at the bottom grew faster than elsewhere. The authors tie it to tight labour markets, and the evidence is mostly correlational. |
| 7. Employers respond with higher pay, cut services or automation | Uncertain | Firms have several options, and automation depends on cost and technology. | No study applies to this premise. The story shows only a small trial, not a takeover. |
| 8. Access to money stays unequal | Plausible | Ambition does not create collateral, schooling or networks. | Six randomised trials found small loans modestly positive but not transformative for the poor. |
| 9. Patience alone does not decide who wins | Plausible | Starting conditions still matter. | A large marshmallow-test replication found the link to later achievement shrank by about two-thirds once family background was controlled. That fraction is my calculation from the paper's figures. |
| 10. Comparison pressure, transactional friendships, health traded for money | Speculative | A dramatic choice that fits the premise. | Not researched as a prediction. It is shown through one character, not stated as what happens to society. |

**Claims the video must not make**

- That poverty or inequality ends.
- That patience or saving alone predicts success.
- That essential workers disappear.
- That small-business owners earn less than employees. (The source only shows that jobs in new firms pay less and offer fewer benefits.)
- Any effect size for financial education. The two main reviews disagree, and one set of figures could not be checked.
- That 2023 is still the record for business applications. Later years were not verified.

**Numbers allowed on screen (at most two):** the US survival figures from link 4, labelled as US data, and nothing else unless a later check confirms it.

## 5. Visual style

The look is a simplified, friendly 3D-style cartoon: big clean shapes, soft light and a small palette. It is built to stay consistent across shots, not to look like a film.

**What changed from v1:** realistic skin, detailed materials, volumetric light and "professional camera moves" are dropped. The palette logic, the colour arc and the one-idea-per-shot discipline stay.

**Style rules**

- **Characters:** about four to five heads tall, large readable eyes and hands, hair and clothes as solid colour shapes with soft shading. No pores, no hair simulation, no fabric detail.
- **Environments:** three clear depth layers (front, middle, back), at most eight hero props per location, simple textures, soft gradients.
- **Light:** one soft key direction per scene, gentle shadows, no heavy haze.
- **Camera:** mostly locked frames, slow pushes or pans, and one clear action per shot.
- **Explainer graphics:** economic ideas (savings, prices, wages) use flat 2D panels in the same palette. They are cheap to make and keep every shot consistent.
- **Text:** captions and numbers are added in the editor, never generated inside the pictures.

**Colour arc** (a toned-down version of v1)

| Beat | Palette | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary life | Warm neutrals, slightly muted | Familiar, tight money |
| The change spreads | Gradual shift toward gold and green | Curiosity, momentum |
| Growth | Warm gold, higher saturation | Optimism |
| Competition | Cooler teal against one warm accent | Tension |
| Consequences | Muted, longer shadows | Weight |
| Ending | Balanced and slightly changed | Reflection |

**Mobile-safe framing (approximate, verify against platform guidance):** keep faces, key action and text inside the middle band, away from the top edge, the bottom caption area and the right-hand button column.

**Fallback rule:** if the proof of concept shows that the 3D-style stills cannot be held consistent in the chosen tools, the style drops to a flat 2D cartoon with the same character designs. Consistency beats dimensionality.

## 6. Three recurring characters

Three characters carry the story, and each represents a different relationship with ambition. They are my own designs, open to revision. Full reference sheets (front, side, three-quarter, expressions, poses) are made in the next stage, after you approve these designs.

**Asha, the primary character (26).** A cafe cashier who starts selling baked snacks from a market stall.

- **Purpose:** the viewer's way in. She shows productive ambition and how it slides toward overwork.
- **Silhouette and build:** round and soft, the shortest of the three.
- **Identity anchors:** a mustard-yellow jacket, short dark curly hair, a small red notebook she writes her sums in.
- **Palette:** jacket #E8A33D, trousers #3B4A5A, notebook #C8493A, hair #2A1D1A.
- **Key expressions:** tired, hopeful, determined, anxious, content.
- **Key poses:** counting coins, kneading dough, writing in the notebook, standing alone at the closed stall.

**Emeka, supporting (52).** A city bus driver.

- **Purpose:** the essential-worker thread. He shows that a rich mentality is not only starting a business, and how wages, hours and automation respond when others leave his trade.
- **Silhouette and build:** tall and broad, with a squared-off outline.
- **Identity anchors:** a navy transit cap, a grey-streaked beard, a steel thermos on the dashboard.
- **Palette:** uniform #26405E, thermos #9AA5AD, beard #8A8A8A, skin #5B3A29.
- **Key expressions:** calm, amused, worried, proud, thoughtful.
- **Key poses:** hands on the wheel, pouring from the thermos, reading a payslip, studying a repair manual.

**Sam, supporting (27).** Asha's best friend and early business partner.

- **Purpose:** the warning, without being a villain. He shows comparison, pressure and relationships turning transactional. He also makes something useful early on.
- **Silhouette and build:** tall, lean and angular, with spiky short hair.
- **Identity anchors:** a charcoal blazer, bright white sneakers, a phone that is always in his hand.
- **Palette:** blazer #3A3F4B, sneakers #F4F4F0, phone glow #7FD6E8, hair #1E1E24.
- **Key expressions:** eager, competitive, distracted, hollow, relieved.
- **Key poses:** checking the phone, pitching, walking fast past someone, sitting alone with the phone face down.

**Relative height:** Emeka 100 percent, Sam about 95 percent, Asha about 88 percent. Their silhouettes (round, square, angular) must stay readable as solid black shapes at thumbnail size.

**Crowds:** simplified figures in the same palette, used only for the market, the bus and the city shots.
