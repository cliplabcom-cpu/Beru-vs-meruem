# Rich Mentality — Module 03: Global Visual Consistency Guide

Applies to all four environments (street, café, market stall, bus). Sits beside `03_Environment_Design_Guide.md`. Does not change any approved Phase 1 or Phase 2 decision.

**Limits of this guide.** The rules below were derived from the Production Bible, Module 01 and the Phase 2 correction report (written specs), and from the reference renders in this folder. I could not view the approved Phase 2 character images, so character-to-environment matching is checked against written specs only (see the final report).

## 1. Lighting

- **Key light:** upper-left-front, camera-relative (direction ≈ −0.55, 0.78, −0.40). This matches the Phase 2 rule that character light stays on the upper left.
- **Interiors:** windows go on the **left** wall so the light source is plausible. If a scene needs windows elsewhere, keep the key direction and treat them as fill.
- **Shadows:** soft, short, grounded contact shadows plus a gentle cast shadow toward lower-right. No hard black shadows.
- **Shading:** flat-shaded faces, warm when lit, cool when in shadow (low contrast). Characters must always read lighter or more saturated than their background.
- **Secondary light:** warm pendant or ceiling lights are accents only.
- **Mood by time:** Day 1 pale morning sky; Year 10 slightly warmer sky. Do not add night or dusk without a story reason.

## 2. Camera language

- One camera model for every scene: perspective, human-scale, mostly **locked-off**. Moves only with a story purpose (slow push-in, gentle pan, walk-along).
- **Heights:** eye level 1.6–1.8 m for people-level shots; 2.3–2.5 m for establishing; no extreme high or low angles, no dutch tilt.
- **Lenses:** fov 58–66 for hero and conversation shots; 74–78 only for tight interiors and wide establishing.
- **Match cuts:** reuse the same camera pose for time passing (A1 → A3 is the template).
- **9:16:** compose for vertical from the start. Keep the story subject in the centre 9:16 window; treat the 16:9 renders as layout references, not final frames.

## 3. Colour harmony

- One muted warm family across all locations: cream, plaster, sand, clay, wood, sage, slate. Low saturation so the characters (saturated clothing colours) pop.
- Accent slots: clay, sage, slate, sand, teal, gold. **Teal** is reserved for competition / digital screens; **gold** for earned success.
- **Colour arc** (from the Bible): warm neutrals → gold and green growth → teal competition → muted dip → balanced. Environments shift only through props, signs and awning colours, never a wholesale re-palette.
- Do not use pure white or pure black in set pieces. White = `#F5F1E8`, ink = `#3E3C44`.

## 4. Object proportions and scale

| Item | Value (m) |
|---|---|
| Emeka / Sam / Asha | 1.85 / 1.76 / 1.63 (100 / 95 / 88 %) |
| Door | 2.02–2.15 |
| Counter | 1.00–1.05 |
| Table / chair seat | 0.76 / 0.50 |
| Market canopy clearance | 2.15 front, 2.45 back |
| Pendant lamp bottom | ≥ 2.10 |
| Bus aisle | 1.06 (stylised wide) |
| Bench seat | 0.46 |
| Curb | 0.12 |

- Shapes are chunky and simplified: boxes, cylinders and spheres with softened edges. No thin or intricate detail.
- Props are slightly oversized relative to realism so they read at phone size, but never bigger than the characters' read-scale.
- The characters' chunky build is accommodated by widening spaces (bus aisle), not by shrinking the characters.

## 5. Materials

- Matte, flat colour. No gloss, no reflections, no photographic textures.
- Glass is pale blue-green and semi-opaque (use `#CADBD4` / `#A9C4C2`).
- Wood = warm mid brown with a darker trim; metal = slate or steel grey, flat.
- Cloth items (awnings, bunting) are two-colour stripes or triangles.

## 6. Background-detail limits

- ≤ 8 hero props per location; 3 depth layers (foreground, mid, background).
- No clutter props "for realism". If a prop doesn't support a story beat, remove it.
- Backgrounds stay simple enough to animate a character in front of them without busy edges.
- **No readable text in generated images.** Signs, menus, price boards, screens, route panels and QR plates are blank or abstract bars. All text and numbers are added in editing, so languages and prices can change without redoing art.

## 7. Character-environment relationship

- Characters stand on blurred contact shadows; their feet never float.
- Do not put character colours in large background areas: Emeka's navy, Sam's charcoal, Asha's amber stay character-only (the bus seats are sage, not navy).
- Reserve clear "stage" floor space at each interaction area (see the identity guide per location).
- Children and teens: not shown in this module's images. Any environment used for them needs a separate scale check (their height and proportion specs belong to Phase 2).

## 8. Premise integrity

- Environments show the world doing normal things: shops, work, transport. They must not show money appearing, free opportunity, or instant results.
- Changes over time (A1 → A3, C1 → C2) must come from visible effort, replication or trade-offs (e.g. a closed shop in Year 10), never from magic.

## 9. Do / Don't checklist for new shots

**Do:** reuse the shared light, palette and camera rules; use existing modules; keep text blank; check scale against the chart.
**Don't:** add new hero props without removing one; change a location's key colours; introduce photoreal textures; use busy backgrounds behind faces.
