## 4. Revised story (only where it changed)

**Day 1 (new).** Across one ordinary day, people begin to plan for more, each in their own way and at their own time. A person slows at a shop window and checks a price. A child drops a coin in a jar. A teenager reads on a phone. One adult paces out a patch of pavement, thinking of starting something. Another walks home from work reading a notebook, planning to be better at the job they already have. Someone turns out an empty pocket: they want more too, and have nothing to spare. Nobody is handed a coin, and most people simply go on with their lives.

**Day 2.** *(was "Day 1")* Asha makes Emeka's usual out of habit. He sets his own thermos on the counter and leaves the cup and pastry untouched. He isn't unkind. He's saving.

**Weeks 1–5.** Asha costs the idea in her notebook. Sam offers: *"I'll cover the flour. You bake. We split the sales."* His pile is bigger than hers. Neither pretends otherwise. The owner shares the oven if she opens early. Batch one burns; batch two works. At the stop, nobody pauses for Sam's tray until a commuter takes **a free taste, and walks on**. A little later **he comes back** and pays. Sam brings Asha that first coin, and she tapes it inside her notebook.

**Month 3.** Some people quit jobs to build things. **Most stay, learn, and ask for more**, so only some shops have trouble finding staff. Buses stay full and Emeka takes extra shifts. He starts studying the buses.

**Month 6–8.** Others copy Asha's snack, and some start with more. One stall closes; not all the ones that close were worse. Emeka hears a sound that is wrong, **reports it, and a mechanic confirms a leak**. The bus is pulled from service. *"Good catch."* (He is a driver who noticed. He does not repair anything.)

**Year 1.** After Sam leaves, Emeka sets down the thermos: *"Tea. Take a minute."* Asha recounts her days **including herself**, and chooses one batch a day, done by noon, a day off a week, and an oven fund.

**Years 3–5.** Sam's evenings empty. **(Kiosk removed.)** Emeka now trains new drivers, for a little more pay.

**Year 10.** Some built. Some moved up. Some kept saving. Emeka buys a snack on purpose. Sam arrives at the edge of the plaza with his phone dark. *"I left you carrying everything. You were right about the regulars."* *"I should've said it was too much. Your numbers weren't wrong."* She hands him a snack. They are not partners again. The coin Sam brought is still in her notebook.

**Final line (spoken in two sentences):** *"Ambition is powerful when it creates value for others. And dangerous when it costs us the people and life we were building for."*

**Character-journey changes.** *Sam:* his ending now includes an explicit admission of leaving Asha with the work. His fear is still treated as reasonable. *Asha:* she acknowledges not speaking up and brushing off his numbers. *Emeka:* his payoff is a reported fault and a training role, and he is never shown as a mechanic.
