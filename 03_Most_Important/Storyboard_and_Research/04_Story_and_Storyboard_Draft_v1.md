## A. Story overview

**One-line story.** On Day 1 every adult starts thinking like a builder, and nobody is given a coin. A café cashier turns one small, specific need into a market stall. Her regular, a bus driver, keeps the buses running and keeps saving. Her best friend slides into comparing himself with everyone else. Ten years later the street looks almost the same, and the people are not.

**Beginning (Day 1 to Month 2, shots 1–7).** A quiet reversal opens the film. Emeka, Asha's regular, skips his usual pastry and puts his own steel thermos on her counter. Across the street, people stop buying little things. The narrator states the premise: every adult now wants what a builder wants, and nobody got a coin. Asha, with an empty café and her red notebook, does sums. She notices that commuters at the bus stop have empty hands: they still need breakfast but won't pay café prices. She and Sam pool what they have (Sam has more) and open a small stall. First sale.

**Middle (Month 3 to Year 1, shots 8–13).** The world gets crowded. Shops hang help-wanted ribbons because many people are busy building something else. Buses still have to run, so Emeka takes extra shifts and studies a repair manual. Six identical stalls sell the same snack, and one has a delivery van. Asha lasts because she is early and Sam's pre-order list means she never bakes blind. Then stalls start closing. Asha pushes herself: a lit window at 2 a.m., and one morning she hands over a snack without looking up.

**Ending (Year 1 to Year 10, shots 14–18).** Sam, now watching other people's numbers, wants to grow faster. Asha says no, because she can see the line of people in front of her. Sam leaves and spends a few years winning on paper and losing evenings. A ticket machine appears at one bus stop on trial, and the bus still has a driver. Ten years on, the street looks almost the same. Some shops are gone, a few are new. At the stall, Emeka buys the treat he skipped on Day 1, this time on purpose. Sam is in the queue with his phone in his pocket. Asha puts the red notebook away and is content.

**Message (proposal).** The middle runs on message B (*a stall lasts because people need what it makes*). The closing line is message A, your preferred direction: *"Ambition is a tool. It builds when it serves people — and hollows out when it replaces them."* The "replaces them" half is carried by Sam (a phone in place of a friend) and by Asha's one lapse (shot 13), not by claims about society.

**What the story deliberately does not say:** that poverty or inequality ends; that patience or saving alone decides success; that essential workers vanish; that founders earn more or less than employees; that wages rose, automation took over, or investing paid off. Those appear only as things we see happening to one person at a time.

**Bible v2's three tests as story logic.** Asha creates value for another person (test 1) and is pushed toward failing "can stop and rest" (test 2) and "room for health and relationships" (test 3) in shots 12–13. She then chooses enough in shot 14. Sam fails tests 2 and 3 early. Neither is a villain, and Sam's list is what saves Asha's stall.

**Research spine used (Module 01, 13.1):** Great Pause, market fills up, same idea different start, help wanted, strained essential service, small automation trial, signs flip to Closed, lights stay on, comparison, same street two states. **Dropped on purpose** to avoid new sets and extras: the loan window, the depot pay board, the "glossy offer" booth, and the savings-app-at-the-kitchen-table scene.

## B. Character roles

**Asha (anchor: productive ambition and its risk).** The viewer's way in and the Human Test at the end. She turns a small unmet need into a business, makes the film's one real decision (saying no to growing faster), and is the person we are left with. Jacket at the café; sage half-apron from the stall onward (approved variation).

**Emeka (essential work, dignity under strain).** Asha's regular and the film's quiet counterweight: a rich mentality is not only starting a business. His thermos is the recurring symbol of saving. He never quits and is never helpless or anti-ambition: he saves, takes extra shifts, studies a repair manual. His strain comes from conditions (crowded buses, long shifts), shown, not claimed. His payoff is the treat he finally chooses to buy.

**Sam (the warning, not a villain).** He contributes real value early: the larger share of the stall money and the pre-order list. He drifts into comparison (the cool phone light grows on his face) and the film shows it as his personal path, not a claim about people in general. We leave him back in the queue, not condemned.

**How they connect**
- **Asha and Emeka:** a counter relationship. First meet in shot 1 (his arm and thermos only). Reunite at the stall in shot 18. His commute is why the stall sits by the bus route.
- **Asha and Sam:** already best friends (established). Partnership starts in shot 6, helps in 10, breaks in 14, mends only in 18.
- **Emeka and Sam:** share no scene until shot 18. That is a weakness (see critique), not an oversight.

## F. Honest critique

1. **The production method is the real risk.** Phase 3.1 itself says the cut-out method may not scale to Emeka and Sam without a proper rig. Six shots lean on exactly that (2, 3, 9, 14, 16, 18). If the method is not accepted, the story needs staging changes, not just more time.
2. **The cab shots do not work as the environment stands.** D2 looks forward from the aisle, so Emeka's approved front view can't sit at that wheel. I fixed it with a reverse angle (new render) and by moving shot 16 outside the bus. A cheaper alternative: show Emeka only from behind in the existing D2 view and carry his feeling through narration and the thermos. That is a story change, so I did not make it.
3. **Pacing.** 18 shots in 2:42 averages 9 seconds a shot, long for animation that can't move much. Mitigation: every shot has an internal beat, but this needs testing in a first animatic.
4. **Too many threads.** Shots 5, 8 and 12 are the weakest. A lean version without them is 15 shots and about 2:22. I would cut 8 first, then 5.
5. **Dialogue-free is risky.** Shot 14 depends on silent acting through front-view heads. If it does not land, the emotional core fails. Fallback: one spoken line.
6. **Sam can read as a villain** if shot 14 is played badly. His early usefulness (shots 6 and 10) protects him; keep it. His return in 18 is now a back view only.
7. **The "world" is small.** The premise is "everyone", but we see three people and a few extras. A two-second opening montage would help, at the cost of more assets.
8. **Emeka's thread is thin.** The repair manual (shots 9, 16) has no payoff beyond the treat. Cut it or add a small payoff.
9. **Emeka and Sam never meet before 18.** A fix: his bus passes Sam in shot 15 and Sam does not board. It adds a bus exterior.
10. **The ending is tidy.** Sam returning to the queue may be too neat. Alternative: leave him absent.
11. **The statistic is imperfect.** The BLS figure counts US establishments with employees; Asha's stall is not one, and the setting is generic. The label scopes it honestly but may feel mismatched. Option: drop the number.
12. **The final line can be misheard.** Module 01 flagged that "replaces them" can sound like automation replacing workers. Shot 16 sits close to that reading. Reword, or move the kiosk away from the ending.
13. **Weak-hook risk.** A thermos on a counter may not hold a viewer in three seconds. Test an alternative opening on the street at the moment everyone pauses.
14. **The apron is mostly invisible.** Behind the counter her apron is hidden, so the "stall variation" shows only in shot 14 (and possibly 18).
15. **Baking location.** The lit window implies she bakes after hours in the café, an unstated arrangement. A home kitchen would be a new location.
16. **The bus is the weakest environment** and not approved as a final look (D1 blocky, D2 washed out). Four shots use the bus.
17. **Vertical framing.** The A1/A3 verticals leave about 40% empty pavement and omit the clock post, shelter, closed shutter and arrivals screen. Several story details need a wider plate.
18. **Unverified wording.** "Every shop wanted help" and "one stop got a ticket machine" illustrate plausible and speculative links. I kept each as something seen, not a claim.
