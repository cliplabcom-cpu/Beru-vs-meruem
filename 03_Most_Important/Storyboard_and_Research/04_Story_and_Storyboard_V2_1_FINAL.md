## 2. The final story

**Day 1.** On an ordinary day, people everywhere begin planning for more, each in their own way and in their own time. A person checks a price card. A child saves a coin. A teenager learns something new. One adult paces out a patch of pavement; another walks home reading a notebook, planning to get better at the job they already have. Someone turns out an empty pocket. Nobody is handed a coin, and most people go on with their lives.

**Day 2.** Asha, a café cashier, makes Emeka's usual out of habit. Emeka, a bus driver, sets his own thermos on the counter, then lifts it away and leaves the cup and pastry untouched. He isn't unkind. He's saving. Sam, her best friend, has a phone and some savings.

**Weeks 1–5.** The café goes quiet; commuters still need breakfast but not café prices. Asha does the sums in her red notebook: cost, price, oven time. Sam offers: *"I'll cover the flour. You bake. We split what's left after costs."* His pile is bigger; neither pretends otherwise. The owner rents her the oven before opening. Batch one burns; batch two works. At the bus stop, nobody stops for Sam's tray until a commuter takes a free taste and walks on. Later he comes back and pays. Sam brings Asha the first coin, and she tapes it into her notebook.

**Month 3–6.** The tray becomes a stall in the plaza. Some people quit to build; most stay and learn. Buses stay full and Emeka starts studying them. Others set up beside Asha, some with more. One stall closes; not all the ones that close were worse.

**Month 8.** Emeka notices a leak under his bus, reports it, and a mechanic confirms it. *"Good catch."* He did not repair anything.

**Months 10–Year 1.** Sam wants a second stall with smaller snacks; Asha says the regulars would notice. Sam starts missing mornings; Asha bakes at three and falls asleep at the counter while a customer leaves. Sam leaves to build bigger. At noon Emeka sets his thermos on her counter beside an empty cup: *"Tea. Take a minute."* The cup steams; he takes the thermos with him. Asha counts herself and decides: *"Enough for today. Done by noon."*

**Years 3–5.** Sam's stalls multiply and his evenings empty. Emeka trains new drivers, for a little more pay.

**Year 10.** The street looks almost the same. Some built, some moved up, some kept saving. Asha was good, early, and lucky. Emeka brings the thermos back and sets it on her counter, and buys a snack on purpose. Sam comes to the edge of the plaza: *"I left you carrying everything. They came back because they trusted us."* *"I should've told you when it became too much. Your numbers weren't wrong."* She holds a snack out to him; he answers with a breath and a small smile. They are not partners again. The first coin is still taped in her notebook.

**Final narration (two sentences):** *"Ambition is powerful when it creates value for others. But dangerous when we lose the people and the life that matter while chasing it."*

**Character journeys.** *Asha:* ambition that works, then costs her sleep; she keeps the ambition and caps the pace, and admits she did not speak up. *Sam:* his fear is reasonable; he leaves her carrying the work, comes back, and admits it. *Emeka:* a skill-based payoff (a reported fault, a training role), never shown as a mechanic.

## 3. All ages vs adults only (assumption A1 in Module 01)

- **Project premise:** everyone. **Bible v2 premise lock:** "every adult." **Module 01:** modelled adults only, and lists children and teenagers as not modelled.
- **How this storyboard reconciles them:** the economic story stays with **adults**; the three leads are adults (Asha 26, Emeka 52, Sam 27 per the Bible). Children and teenagers appear **only** through age-appropriate habits (saving a coin, learning a skill) in shots 2, 3 and 54, and never as earners, founders or workers. No child or teen has a storyline, and no one younger than adult helps at a stall (V2's teen helper in shot 33 stays removed).
- **Opening wording** avoids universal claims about young people: "**Some** children began saving." "**Some** teenagers started learning new skills."
- **Not edited:** the Bible and Module 01. Suggested later wording for both, for your decision: *"Overnight, people of every age adopt age-appropriate versions of these habits; the economic mechanisms and the three leads are adults."*

## 8. Remaining genuine animation risks

1. **P4: the raised-arm sleeve.** Emeka's art has the arm hanging down. It is used four times (9, 47, 48, 56), including two lift-aways. If it fails, shots 9, 47, 48 and 56 each need a different treatment, and the thermos return in 56 is the one the ending depends on.
2. **Side glides read as sliding** (36, 45, 57). Shot 57 also changes scale with the feet.
3. **Sam has no warm or relieved head.** His shot-61 reply uses Reflective → Confident; the half-smile may read as smug. Emeka has Relieved; Sam does not.
4. **Front-view-only acting.** Every listening face is front, and eyelines to off-screen speakers are implied by cutting. Shot 52's silhouette nod stands in for an eyeline Emeka cannot make.
5. **The leak is a flat graphic** (36, 37). D3's bus is small and the characters stand in the lower paving band at a larger scale, so the patch is not perspective-correct. The drip insert carries the meaning.
6. **Silhouette work (P6)** and **time-of-day grades (P8)** are untested at 9:16.
7. **The opening may feel like a slideshow:** six cuts in 15.5 s. "Worldwide" comes from the narration, because only approved environments are used.
8. **The bus look is unapproved** (D1 is used only in shot 30; Emeka is about 200 px tall there).
9. **Emeka and Sam never share a scene.**
10. **Narration is untested by voice.** The tightest lines (about 2.7 words per second) are listed in the QC result below.
11. **Not tested at all:** no animation, grade, overlay, sound or voice has been run. All size and position figures are estimates by eye.
