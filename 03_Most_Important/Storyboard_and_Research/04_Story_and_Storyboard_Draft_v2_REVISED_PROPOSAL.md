## B. The complete revised story

**Day 1.** One morning, everyone on Earth starts thinking like a builder, children and teenagers included, in age-appropriate ways. A child counts coins into a jar. A teenager copies a tutorial into a notebook. An adult paces out an empty gap between market stalls. Some people have savings on a phone. Some have a few coins. Some have nothing to spare, and they want more all the same. Nobody is handed a coin.

We meet **Asha**, a café cashier. Every morning **Emeka**, a bus driver, buys a coffee and a pastry. Today Asha makes his usual out of habit. He gently pushes it back and sets his own steel thermos on the counter. He isn't unkind. He's saving. By the end of the week the café is quiet. **Sam**, Asha's best friend, has a phone, some savings and a plan.

**Weeks 1–4.** Asha watches commuters at the bus stop outside the café. They still need breakfast; they just won't pay café prices now. She does the sums in her red notebook: what a snack costs to make, what a commuter might pay without thinking twice, the cost of oven time. She can't afford to start. Sam offers his savings and says he knows the whole stop. His pile of coins is bigger than hers, and neither pretends otherwise. Sam buys flour in bulk. The café owner agrees to let Asha use the oven before opening, if she opens early. Asha bakes at four in the morning. Her first batch burns. A week of flour is gone. She writes it down and bakes again. The second batch comes out right.

**Week 5.** Sam sets a tray on a crate at the stop. Nobody stops. Then a commuter takes a free taste, and comes back with a coin. Through the café window Asha sees it. She tapes that first coin inside her notebook cover.

**Month 3.** The tray becomes a market stall with a second-hand canopy. For a while it works. Asha bakes, Sam sells. Around them the world gets crowded: shops can't find staff because everyone is building something else; buses are packed, and Emeka takes extra shifts. He starts studying the buses, the way he always wondered about.

**Month 6–8.** Every stall now sells the same snack. The one beside Asha's has more helpers and more stock, and a longer line. Sam starts glancing sideways at their numbers. One stall closes. Not all of the stalls that close were worse than Asha's. Meanwhile Emeka hears a sound he has been studying for. His bus comes off the road before it can fail. The depot radio says: "Good catch."

**Month 9–Year 1.** Sam wants a second stall and smaller snacks to protect the margin. Asha says people would notice, and they are the reason the stall exists. Sam starts missing mornings. Asha fills in: baking at three, serving until dark. One day she dozes at the counter with a customer waiting, and the customer walks away. Sam leaves to build something bigger. Asha is alone, exhausted.

At noon Emeka, on his layover, sets his thermos on her counter: "Drink. Then sit." The thermos that once meant *saving* now means *looking after someone*. Asha does the sums again, this time counting herself. She caps the batch, closes by noon, takes one day a week off, and sketches in her notebook the small oven she means to save for.

**Years 3–5.** Sam's stalls multiply; his evenings empty. We see him alone at the bus stop, phone glowing, then dark. At the depot, short of drivers, a ticket machine is trialled at the busiest stop. The buses still need someone who can hear when something is wrong. Emeka now trains new drivers, for a little more pay.

**Year 10.** The street looks almost the same. Some had built something. Some were still saving. Asha's stall lasted: she was good, early and lucky. Emeka sets down his thermos and buys the snack on purpose, with money he saved. Sam appears at the edge of the plaza with his phone dark. "You were right about the line." "I was scared of the numbers too." Asha hands him a snack. They are not partners again. The first coin is still taped inside her notebook.

**Closing line (proposed):** *"Ambition is powerful when it creates value for others — and dangerous when it costs us the people and the life we were building for."*

**What the story does not say:** that poverty or inequality ends; that everyone can save, invest or reach a bank; that every business survives; that starting a business beats a job; that essential workers vanish or machines replace them; that patience or mindset alone explains who succeeds.

## C. Character journeys

### Asha (26, café cashier → stall owner)
- **Start:** Competent, quiet, short of money. Notebook keeper. Her café sales vanish in Week 1.
- **Goal:** Make something people will pay for, on her own terms.
- **Decisions:** Cost the idea honestly. Accept Sam's savings and the owner's early-morning oven deal. Re-bake after failure. Refuse smaller snacks (Month 10). Cap her output and her hours (Year 1).
- **Conflict:** Capital (needs a partner); skill (burnt batch); competition (a better-stocked neighbour); herself (overwork, dozing, a lost customer, a lost friend).
- **Emotional development:** Hopeful → cautious → determined → exhausted → alone → *deliberately content*. Her low point is physical (dozing) and relational (Sam leaves).
- **End:** One stall, a daily limit, a day off, an oven fund. Still ambitious. Reconciled, but not partnered, with Sam.

### Emeka (52, bus driver)
- **Start:** A daily coffee-and-pastry customer at Asha's café. Steady job, no spare money.
- **Goal:** Keep his job, keep saving, understand his own trade better.
- **Decisions:** Pushes his usual back and brings a thermos (Day 1). Takes extra shifts. Studies the buses. Reports the fault. Teaches others. Gives Asha tea when she's exhausted. Buys a treat on purpose.
- **Conflict:** Crowded buses and short staffing. A new ticket machine that could feel like a threat. He treats it as a tool.
- **Emotional development:** Calm → tired → curious → proud → generous → quietly content. He is never helpless and never anti-ambition.
- **End:** Still a driver, now a trainer, a little better paid. He spends coins he saved, on something he chose.

### Sam (27, Asha's best friend)
- **Start:** Eager, has a phone and some savings. Knows the bus stop.
- **Goal:** Build something; not be left behind.
- **Decisions:** Puts up more money than Asha. Sells at the stop and wins the first sale with a free taste. Starts counting rivals. Pushes for expansion. Skips mornings. Leaves. Returns.
- **Conflict:** His fear of falling behind, set against Asha's loyalty to her regulars. His data is real; his focus narrows.
- **Emotional development (seven stages):** supportive → partnership works → glances at others' numbers → growth talk → absent mornings → leaves → alone with a dark phone → returns and apologises. His fear is understandable throughout; he is not a villain.
- **End:** Friendship **partially repaired**. He has built something and paid for it in evenings. Whether he rejoins Asha is left open.

## E. Emotional progression

| Stage | Shots | What the viewer should feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening** | 1–6 | *Awe and curiosity*, then a pang of fairness: not everyone can act on this. The world has shifted, and it is not uniform. |
| **Three lives** | 7–12 | *Warmth and puzzlement* at the thermos, then understanding. Small, human, a little sad. |
| **Development** | 13–28 | *Hope and effort.* The audience works for the first sale with Asha: sums, a debt to Sam, a burnt batch, a quiet morning. Small victories feel earned. |
| **Middle** | 29–38 | *Unease, then respect.* The world gets crowded and uneven. Emeka's catch is a quiet lift, proof that skill and care count. |
| **Conflict** | 39–50 | *Tension, then ache.* Sam's drift and Asha's collapse hurt because both are reasonable. Emeka's thermos is the turn: someone looks after her. Her decision is a relief, not a lecture. |
| **Years later** | 51–55 | *Loneliness and acceptance.* Sam's dark phone; the machine and the man; the street unchanged, some still saving. |
| **Ending** | 56–63 | *Bittersweet satisfaction.* The treat on purpose; the apology; the coin. Closing line lands as a quiet summing-up, not a moral. |

**Silence beats:** after shot 20 (the burnt tray), shots 43–44 (the dozing), and the last second before the closing line. Music stays sparse and low under the voice. **Colour arc** (grade and props, as in the Bible): warm neutrals (1–12) → gold and green (23–28) → cooler tone, one warm accent (33–34) → muted (35, 41–46) → balanced and slightly changed (54–63).

## I. Honest critique

**Is V2 better than V1?** Yes, on most of what you listed. The opening now states the premise in the first fifteen seconds. Asha's start follows from observation, costing, shared savings and a failure, instead of a jump. Emeka's learning has a payoff and the thermos has a stated meaning at each use. Sam's decline has stages, and the ending leaves a real relationship, not a queue. The US statistic is gone.

**Remaining weaknesses**
1. **Pacing.** 63 shots in 179 s averages 2.8 s a shot. Many are inserts, so the film may feel like a sequence of stills. Only an animatic will tell. If it does, the lean cut helps.
2. **Sam's decline is still compressed.** Seven stages in about 15 shots, carried partly by Day stamps. A faithful gradual arc would need 20–30 more seconds.
3. **Acting without moving heads.** The argument, the apology and Asha's decision depend on front-view faces and off-screen voices. If P1 or P2 shows the faces are lifeless, the emotional core weakens.
4. **Emeka and Sam never meet.** I did not add a scene because it would need two characters in one frame. This is acceptable but a missed beat.
5. **Emeka's payoff is modest by design.** Some viewers may find a bus catch and a little more pay too small. I think it is right for the research, but it is a judgement.
6. **Kiosk logic is thin.** One line and two shots. It reads as "depot short of drivers"; if you find it confusing, cutting shots 52 and 53 costs little.
7. **Narration pacing.** Most narration is spoken at under 2.7 words per second, but several lines are tight and need a voice test.
8. **Everything depends on assets I have not seen.** The three **[verify]** items and the bus look could each force small changes.
9. **The "lucky" line** is honest, but it could read as modest to a viewer who expects a stronger payoff for Asha's decision. Her own decision is visible (shots 49–50), so I kept it.

**What I did not do:** animate anything, test any rig, generate images or audio, or touch the repository.
